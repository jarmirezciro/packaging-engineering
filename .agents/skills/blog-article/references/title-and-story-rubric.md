# Title and Story Rubric

The article must not begin drafting with the first acceptable title or a generic structure.

## Editorial angle

Before drafting, define:

```text
Reader problem:
Apparent answer:
Surprising result:
Business consequence:
Remaining decision:
Article promise:
```

The story should be driven by one verified tension.

## Useful narrative patterns

Choose the best fit. Do not force every article into the same pattern.

### Hidden improvement

```text
Expected result → overlooked alternative → higher capacity → business consequence
```

### Attractive but weaker proposal

```text
Visually appealing result → downstream evaluation → unexpected penalty
```

### Same result, different decision

```text
Equal numerical performance → operational differences → shortlist for testing
```

### Upstream decision changes the flow

```text
Package choice → pallet effect → transport effect → business outcome
```

### Constraint changes the answer

```text
Simple theoretical answer → real restriction → feasible result
```

## Title laboratory

Generate at least six titles across several styles.

### Result-led

- Same Box, 20% More Products
- From 9,600 to 14,400 Products per Container

### Problem-led

- Why the First Box Design Was Not the Best Logistics Choice
- The Orientation Mistake That Reduced Box Capacity

### Business-led

- How Box Design Changes Pallet and Transport Performance
- How Product Rotation Can Reduce the Number of Boxes Required

### Curiosity-led

- The Box That Left 4,800 Products Behind
- Why Two Efficient Boxes Still Need Different Decisions

### SEO-led

- How Many Products Fit in a Box?
- How Box Design Affects Pallet and Container Capacity

## Score every candidate

Score 1–5:

| Criterion | Test |
|---|---|
| Clarity | Is the topic immediately understandable? |
| Specificity | Does it use a real result or decision? |
| Curiosity | Is there a reason to continue reading? |
| Business relevance | Does the consequence matter beyond geometry? |
| SEO naturalness | Does it support search intent without sounding forced? |
| Credibility | Is it accurate and proportionate? |

Select separate strings for:

- Visible title.
- SEO title.
- Thumbnail headline.

The visible title may be more engaging. The SEO title may be more descriptive. The thumbnail headline must be shorter.

## Opening hook

The strongest result should appear in the first 100 words.

Avoid:

- Generic packaging trends.
- Definitions.
- Tool feature lists.
- “In today's fast-paced world.”
- Long setup before the result.

Prefer:

> A product appeared to fit 15 times in the box. KolliPack found room for 18 without changing the product or box dimensions.

## Story continuity

Every section must advance one of these:

- Problem.
- Comparison.
- Result.
- Business consequence.
- Decision.
- Reader action.

Remove or shorten sections that only demonstrate technical sophistication.

## Repetition control

The core result may appear:

- In the opening.
- In the main comparison.
- In the takeaway.

Do not repeat the same number or disclaimer after every image.

## Ending

The conclusion should:

- Resolve the article promise.
- State what the result changes.
- Clarify what remains to be tested.
- Invite the reader to open the prepopulated case.

Do not merely summarize every heading.
