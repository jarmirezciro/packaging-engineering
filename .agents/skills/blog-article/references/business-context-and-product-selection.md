# Business Context and Product Selection

A case becomes understandable when the reader can picture the product, company need, distribution flow, and decision.

Use the author's `discussion.txt` as the primary source.

## Context to establish

Use the documented facts that help explain the decision:

- Business type.
- Representative product category.
- Customer or buyer type.
- Distribution or handling context.
- Packaging requirement.
- Decision being evaluated.
- Business consequence.

Some fields may be unknown or irrelevant. An evidence-based comparison can be adequately grounded in the documented product, constraints and engineering decision.

## Representative product first

If the source does not identify a product category, use the observed product shape, dimensions and constraints first. Introduce a plausible category only when it improves understanding, and identify it as illustrative.

Examples:

- A rigid household-care retail carton.
- A rectangular automotive spare part.
- A boxed personal-care product.
- A compact electronic component pack.
- A rigid medical-supply carton.

Use wording such as:

> To make the dimensions easier to visualize, consider a rigid rectangular household-care pack of a similar size.

Do not imply that the article concerns a specific real company unless the author supplied that fact.

## Real comparable products

Limited research may be used when a real comparable product materially improves understanding.

Requirements:

- Prefer manufacturer or retailer specifications.
- Cite the source.
- State that it is a comparable example.
- State that KolliPack is not affiliated with the company.
- Do not treat approximate dimensions as exact.
- Do not inherit unsupported weight, fragility, material, or operational assumptions.

## Never fabricate

Do not invent:

- Company or customer names.
- Contracts.
- Retailer requirements.
- Annual volume.
- Freight price.
- Packaging cost.
- Damage rate.
- Compression strength.
- Supplier quotation.
- Confirmed sustainability saving.
- Proven handling performance.

## Illustrative calculations

An assumed volume may be used to explain scale when clearly labeled.

Acceptable:

> At an illustrative volume of 144,000 products, the calculated capacities correspond to 15 versus 10 container loads.

Not acceptable:

> The company saves five containers every year.

The second statement presents an assumed scenario as fact.

## Product plausibility check

Before selecting a product category, consider:

- Dimensions.
- Weight.
- Rigidity.
- Orientation permissions.
- Fragility.
- Stackability.
- Quantity per package.
- Packaging level.
- Distribution channel.

Do not choose a product whose known handling constraints conflict with the case assumptions.

## Business-context quality test

A non-expert should understand:

1. What is being packed or compared.
2. Which verified constraints matter.
3. What decision the investigation informs.
4. Why the numerical result matters.

Keep the context concise. The article remains about the packaging decision, not a fictional company biography.
