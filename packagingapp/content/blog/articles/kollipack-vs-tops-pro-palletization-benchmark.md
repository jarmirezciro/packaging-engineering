## Why one visible case is worth comparing

Palletization software is often assessed through an everyday question: how many cartons fit on the pallet? That question matters, but it is incomplete. A pallet pattern also has to be understandable to the people building it, compatible with handling equipment, and suitable for the carton and transport conditions.

This example is useful because the starting conditions and the leading result are visible in the public TOPS Pro walkthrough. It gives packaging engineers, operations managers, and packaging buyers one concrete way to compare the output of a **48 × 40 pallet calculator** without turning a single example into a broad claim about either product.

## Same best result, different search breadth

This is where the comparison becomes more useful than a simple tie. The supplied TOPS Pro table contains **14 candidate rows**. KolliPack displays a shorter shortlist of **5 options**. Both recover the 50-carton maximum, and both show leading **45-carton** alternatives.

More variants can help when equipment, warehouse practice, or customer handling rules favor a particular arrangement. A shorter shortlist makes the first review faster.

Neither approach is automatically better. Candidate count is not a quality score. It is a prompt for the next question: which of the available patterns fits the carton, equipment, wrapping method, and handling process best?

KolliPack makes that shortlist explicit. Option 2 (**Block**) and Option 3 (**Centre-filled pinwheel**) each carry **45 cartons**, or 9 per layer. Option 4 (**Hybrid pinwheel**) carries **40 cartons**, or 8 per layer, while Option 5 (**Pinwheel**) carries **30 cartons**, or 6 per layer.

The last two cards are not direct pattern pairs. TOPS Pro Alternatives 4 and 5 each show **9 cartons per layer across 5 layers**—45 cartons—while KolliPack Options 4 and 5 show 8 and 6 per layer. In this case, the TOPS Pro variants raise practical stability and handling questions. KolliPack's shortlist favors Brick, Row, Splitrow, Pinwheel, and Hybrid Pinwheel. Physical validation still decides the released pallet pattern.

## Interlock is a decision, not a certificate

Column and interlocked patterns distribute carton contacts differently. That can affect how a pallet responds to wrapping, turns, vibration, compression, and manual or automated handling. But a geometry result alone does not certify a load for shipment.

The final choice still depends on carton strength, pallet condition, stretch-wrap or strapping practice, handling equipment, transport profile, and any customer or carrier requirements. The value of the comparison is not a stability verdict; it is a clearer starting point for the physical checks that matter after the geometry has narrowed the options.

The two detail views are similar in purpose but different in vocabulary. Both describe the same leading case with 10 cartons per layer across 5 layers, and both expose layout information for review rather than certifying physical stability. TOPS presents the arrangement as an interlock and reports its own area and cubic efficiency measures. KolliPack separates the main and alternate layer views, reports 1,155 mm carton arrangement height and 1,256 mm total palletized-load height, and shows pallet floor usage of 91.72% and stack volume usage of 85.09%. It also makes the alternate-layer status visible and leaves the bottom-carton load check clearly marked “Not checked”. Those details make the review more operationally explicit, but the two applications’ percentages and labels should not be treated as like-for-like measures.

The KolliPack report records the case: carton and pallet dimensions, the selected 50-carton arrangement, the calculated **1,256 mm** total height, and the ranked alternatives. It helps move a review into operations or test planning.

Both tools can export a report, but they give the reviewer different control over the output. KolliPack uses a standard report that brings together inputs, the pallet result, ranked patterns, detailed analysis, utilization, and a validation note. TOPS can customize how many metrics are included in the export, so the report can be made narrower or more detailed for a particular review. The supplied examples make that distinction visible: KolliPack emphasizes a consistent review record, while TOPS gives more control over the metric set.

## What this benchmark shows

For this one case, KolliPack matched the best visible quantity from TOPS Pro: 50 cartons, 10 per layer, and 5 layers. TOPS Pro showed a broader set of supplied candidate rows. KolliPack offered a smaller interactive shortlist with column and interlock inspection for the leading arrangement.

That is a useful benchmark result, not proof of full product equivalence. A different carton, pallet footprint, height limit, or operational constraint may change both the best quantity and the pattern choices worth reviewing.
