Product Pallet Optimization

Objective: Show that Kollipack has parity to TOPS when it comes to pallet optimization.

Discussion: 

TOPS uses an optimizer where you can select 5 different quantities, in this case 8,10, 16, 18, or 24, after analysis TOPS concludes that 16 products/box optimize pallet utilization.

KolliPack takes a different approach: instead of automatically selecting a single solution, it allows users to inspect suggested quantities such as 8, 10, 16, 18, and 24 individually. This reflects KolliPack's design philosophy rather than a limitation of the optimization engine. KolliLabs wants users to interact with the tool, explore the data, compare alternatives, adjust variables, and progressively fine-tune their solutions.

For design problems that require optimization across multiple packaging steps, KolliPack provides a downstream optimizer for Bag and Box Calculator in Design Model. It evaluates the different packing alternatives generated in Design Mode and ranks them according to how many base product units can ultimately be accommodated at the final step of the logistics flow.

In contrast to TOPS, Kollipacks packaging flow is modular and customizable, therefore you can create more steps and combinations that you have with TOPS. In addition, TOPS does not offer a solution for Bag or Flexible packaging something that make Kollipack unique, for self supporting products a bag is a strong alternative that can be cheaper than a case for example.

In this case because we have not developed unit selection for Kollipack (International and Imperial units) numbers from the case have been transformed to metric units and rounded up. TOPS uses cardboard flutes to calculate calipers, Kollipack has a manual input for kalliper and therefore calculate box external dimensions. In the case Tops uses C-Flute and for Kollipack we use 4 mm.

When it comes to results, and comparing 8 and 16 units per box, we see that Kollipack delivers similar solutions than TOPS
